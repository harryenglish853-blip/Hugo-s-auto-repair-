What "alignment" means

A wheel alignment adjusts the angles of your wheels so they point the way the vehicle's design intends, relative to each other and to the road. The three angles you'll hear about most:

Toe
Whether the fronts of the tires point slightly inward or outward when viewed from above.
Camber
How far the top of each tire tilts in or out when viewed from the front.
Caster
The forward or backward tilt of the steering axis, which affects steering feel and stability.

Why it drifts out

Curbs, potholes, road debris and normal wear on steering and suspension parts can gradually change these angles. New tires or steering and suspension work are also common times to have alignment looked at.

Only an inspection on alignment equipment can tell you whether your vehicle's angles are within specification. Call Hugo's to set up a time.

Signs an alignment inspection may be a good idea

  • The vehicle drifts or pulls to one side on a straight, level road
  • The steering wheel sits off-center while driving straight
  • Tires are wearing faster on one edge than the other
  • You recently hit a curb, pothole or road debris
  • You've had new tires or steering or suspension parts installed
